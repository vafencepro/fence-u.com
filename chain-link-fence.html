What gauge chain link do commercial projects use?

Most commercial and industrial perimeter work is quoted with 9-gauge fabric — it is the standard for durability against daily wear, equipment contact, and fence-line abuse. Residential and light-duty jobs commonly run 11 or 11½-gauge, and high-security or heavy industrial sites step up to 6-gauge. Heights run from 3 feet up to 12 feet for standard systems, with barbed-wire-ready terminal hardware available for secure perimeters.

If a bid spec calls out a coating class or ASTM standard, match it from the manufacturer documentation — the Master Halco spec sheets in our resource library carry the fabric, framework, and coating data engineers and inspectors ask for.

Galvanized or polymer-coated — which should you quote?

Galvanized fabric and framework is the workhorse: lowest cost, longest track record, right answer for utility, industrial, and most commercial perimeter jobs. Polymer-coated systems (typically black or green over a galvanized core) are the spec for schools, parks, apartment communities, and anywhere the fence has to disappear into the property line. Both come through our Master Halco supply lines with matching fittings, so a color job doesn’t mean mixing suppliers.

What drives wholesale chain link pricing?

Five inputs move the number: fabric gauge and coating, fence height, framework schedule, gate count, and total footage. Steel is a commodity, so market pricing moves — which is exactly why account holders quote against current contractor pricing instead of a stale retail sheet. Send the take-off — run length, heights, terminal and gate locations, and any coating or spec callouts — and the quote comes back as a complete system, priced once, ready to bid.
